## notes

The second Leehom Wang track here, and one whose title is a fixed idiom rather
than a phrase he invented. 天涯海角 — "the edge of the sky, the corner of the
sea" — means the ends of the earth, and it is old enough to appear in classical
poetry. You have already met half of it: 天之涯 turns up in [[song-bie]],
written in 1915.

**Under copyright, so no lyrics here.** Use the video and read the words on a

Four-character idioms like this one are called 成語, and they are a whole
register of the language. Learning them individually is slow going; meeting them
inside songs is much easier.

## listen for

A wide, open arrangement suited to the imagery — plenty of reverb, strings, a
sense of distance.

Wang's diction stays clear even as the arrangement grows. Compare it with
[[xuyao-ren-pei]], which is far sparser, to hear how much production changes the
difficulty of following the same singer.

Listen for the four-syllable rhythm of the title phrase when it lands. 成語 have
a characteristic even cadence, and hearing that shape helps you spot others.
