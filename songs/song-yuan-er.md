## meaning

Seeing a friend off to a garrison at the western frontier. Three lines set the scene and the fourth lands the whole weight: past Yang Pass there will be nobody left who knows him.

## notes

Set to music as 阳关三叠 (*Yángguān Sān Dié*, "Three Refrains on Yang Pass"), one of
the oldest surviving Chinese art songs, usually performed on guqin. A farewell poem
for a friend posted to a garrison at the far western frontier — Yang Pass was the
last outpost before the desert.

Four lines, and the whole weight of the poem lands on the last one: past this point
there is no one left who knows you.

## lines

渭城朝雨浥轻尘
Wèi chéng zhāo yǔ yì qīng chén
Morning rain in Weicheng settles the light dust

客舍青青柳色新
Kè shè qīng qīng liǔ sè xīn
By the inn the willows are green, their color new

劝君更尽一杯酒
Quàn jūn gèng jìn yī bēi jiǔ
Let me urge one more cup of wine on you

西出阳关无故人
Xī chū Yáng guān wú gù rén
West of Yang Pass there will be no old friends

## vocab

朝雨 | zhāo yǔ | morning rain
轻尘 | qīng chén | light dust
客舍 | kè shè | inn, guest house
柳 | liǔ | willow (traditional symbol of parting — 柳 liǔ puns on 留 liú, "to stay")
劝 | quàn | to urge, to persuade
一杯酒 | yī bēi jiǔ | a cup of wine
阳关 | Yáng guān | Yang Pass, the western frontier gate
故人 | gù rén | an old friend
