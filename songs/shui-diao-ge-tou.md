## notes

Written at the Mid-Autumn Festival in 1076, while Su Shi was drunk, far from home,
and missing his brother. Nine hundred years later it is still what people quote at
the Mid-Autumn Festival.

The poem text is ancient and free to reproduce. Note that the famous *sung* versions
use modern melodies that are themselves under copyright — the words below are Su
Shi's; the tune you'll hear on the recording belongs to its composer.

The last two lines are the ones everyone knows. If you learn nothing else here,
learn those.

## lines

明月几时有
Míng yuè jǐ shí yǒu
When did the bright moon first appear?

把酒问青天
Bǎ jiǔ wèn qīng tiān
Wine cup in hand, I ask the blue sky

不知天上宫阙
Bù zhī tiān shàng gōng què
I wonder — in those palaces of heaven

今夕是何年
Jīn xī shì hé nián
what year is it tonight?

我欲乘风归去
Wǒ yù chéng fēng guī qù
I long to ride the wind and return there

又恐琼楼玉宇
Yòu kǒng qióng lóu yù yǔ
yet I fear those towers of jade and marble

高处不胜寒
Gāo chù bù shèng hán
are too cold to bear, so high up

起舞弄清影
Qǐ wǔ nòng qīng yǐng
I rise and dance with my own clear shadow

何似在人间
Hé sì zài rén jiān
what place could match this world of ours?

人有悲欢离合
Rén yǒu bēi huān lí hé
People know sorrow and joy, parting and reunion

月有阴晴圆缺
Yuè yǒu yīn qíng yuán quē
the moon knows dark and clear, waxing and waning

此事古难全
Cǐ shì gǔ nán quán
these things have never once been perfect

但愿人长久
Dàn yuàn rén cháng jiǔ
I wish only that we live long

千里共婵娟
Qiān lǐ gòng chán juān
and share this moon a thousand miles apart

## vocab

明月 | míng yuè | bright moon
青天 | qīng tiān | the blue sky, the heavens
乘风 | chéng fēng | to ride the wind
不胜寒 | bù shèng hán | unable to bear the cold
悲欢离合 | bēi huān lí hé | sorrow and joy, parting and reunion (set phrase for life's ups and downs)
圆缺 | yuán quē | waxing and waning
但愿 | dàn yuàn | if only, I wish that
千里 | qiān lǐ | a thousand li, i.e. a great distance
婵娟 | chán juān | the moon (poetic); a beautiful woman
