## notes

A collaboration between OSN and Dizzy Dizzo 蔡詩芸, one of the few prominent
women rapping in Mandarin. Two voices, two registers, and a good deal of
code-switching between them.

**Under copyright, so no lyrics here.** Use the video and read the words on a
licensed service.

Like [[ai-wo-de-shihou]], the duet structure is an advantage for listening
practice — the handovers give the ear somewhere to reset, and hearing the same
language from two very different voices helps separate the sounds of the
language from the habits of one singer.

## listen for

Dizzy Dizzo's delivery is harder-edged and more percussive than OSN's; she lands
on consonants where he slides between vowels. Their contrast is the interest of
the track.

The tide metaphor is worth a note. 潮 means tide, but it also means fashionable
or trendy in modern slang — 潮流 is a trend, and calling something 很潮 means it
is cool. That double sense is part of why the title works in Chinese.

Roughly mid-tempo with a heavy beat. Denser than [[circles]], lighter than
[[so-bad]].
