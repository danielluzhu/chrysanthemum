## notes

The Chinese folk song best known outside China. A British envoy's attaché
transcribed it in the 1790s and published it in London; Puccini later borrowed the
melody for *Turandot*. If you have heard one Chinese folk tune, it is this one.

Simple, repetitive, and slow — the best possible first song for a learner. The
grammar is plain and the vocabulary is everyday.

## lines

好一朵美丽的茉莉花
Hǎo yī duǒ měilì de mòlihuā
What a beautiful jasmine flower

芬芳美丽满枝桠
Fēnfāng měilì mǎn zhīyā
Fragrant and lovely, crowding every branch

又香又白人人夸
Yòu xiāng yòu bái rénrén kuā
Sweet-scented and white, praised by everyone

让我来将你摘下
Ràng wǒ lái jiāng nǐ zhāi xià
Let me pick you from the bough

送给别人家
Sòng gěi biérén jiā
and give you to another

茉莉花呀茉莉花
Mòlihuā ya mòlihuā
Jasmine flower, oh jasmine flower

## vocab

茉莉花 | mòlihuā | jasmine flower
一朵 | yī duǒ | one (measure word for flowers and clouds)
美丽 | měilì | beautiful
芬芳 | fēnfāng | fragrant, sweet-smelling
枝桠 | zhīyā | branch, bough
又…又… | yòu … yòu … | both … and … (pattern)
夸 | kuā | to praise
摘 | zhāi | to pick, to pluck
