## notes

A duet with 單依純, a singer who broke through on a televised competition and has
one of the more distinctive voices in current Mandopop — deeper and huskier than
the genre's norm.

**Under copyright, so no lyrics here.** Use the video and read the words on a
licensed service.

Duets are underrated as listening practice. Two voices in different registers
singing the same language give you two chances at every phrase, and the
handover points give the ear somewhere to rest.

## listen for

Track the alternation. Chou takes one section, 單依純 answers, and they join for
the choruses. Where they overlap the words get harder to separate — that is
normal, and worth noticing rather than fighting.

Her lower register means her consonants land differently from his. Comparing the
same word sung by both is a genuinely useful exercise for hearing past a
particular singer's habits to the underlying sounds.

The title is a time clause — "when you loved me" — and the whole song sits in
that frame, looking at a period that has ended.

## patterns

…的時候 | when … — the standard time-clause frame, clause goes first | 我小的时候 | when I was little
曾經 | once, formerly — marks a completed period, often wistful | 我们曾经很快乐 | We were happy once
那時候 | at that time, back then | 那时候我还不懂 | Back then I still didn't understand
Verb + 著 | ongoing state or manner — continuous aspect | 他笑着说 | He said, smiling
不再 + verb | no longer | 我不再等了 | I'm not waiting any longer
