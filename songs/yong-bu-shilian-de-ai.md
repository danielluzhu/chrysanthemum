## meaning

A love that never goes out of contact. 失聯 is the word used for a missing aircraft, which makes the promise in the title more absolute than the English suggests.

## notes

One of Eric Chou's largest hits, and a good companion to
[[yihou-bie-zuo-pengyou]] — same voice, same piano-led register, but where that
song is resigned, this one insists.

**Under copyright, so no lyrics here.** Use the video and read the words on a
licensed service.

The title rewards unpacking before you listen. 失聯 means to lose contact — the
word used for a missing aircraft or someone who has dropped off the map — and
永不 negates it absolutely. So: a love that never goes out of contact. That is a
distinctly modern image, and it tells you what the song is reaching for.

## listen for

Piano and voice for the opening, strings entering underneath, a full arrangement
by the final chorus. The dynamic range is wide; played quietly you will miss
how much the ending opens up.

Chou's phrasing is unhurried and he places consonants clearly, so this is one of
the better tracks on the playlist for training your ear. Follow the ends of
phrases in particular — he holds final syllables long enough that you can catch
the tone even when the melody is pulling against it.

The chorus repeats with an added layer each time rather than changing. Use the
first pass to get the shape, the second to catch words, the third to check.

## patterns

永不 + verb | never, not ever — absolute and emphatic, more literary than 从不 | 永不放弃 | Never give up
失聯 | to lose contact — used of people and of aircraft alike | 我们失联很久了 | We lost touch a long time ago
就算…也… | even if … , still … — concession | 就算下雨，我也会去 | Even if it rains, I'll still go
舍不得 | can't bear to part with — no neat English equivalent | 我舍不得离开 | I can't bear to leave
一辈子 | a whole lifetime — very common in love songs | 我想陪你一辈子 | I want to be with you for a lifetime
