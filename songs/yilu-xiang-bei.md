## notes

The second Jay Chou track here, and a very different one from
[[gaobai-qiqiu]]. Where that song is light and playful, this is a slow ballad —
and it sits closer in mood to [[ju-hua-tai]], though in plain modern Mandarin
rather than Vincent Fang's archaic register.

**Under copyright, so no lyrics here.** Use the video and read the words on a
licensed service.

The title is a compact travel phrase: 一路 ("the whole way") + 向北 ("towards the
north"). 向 marks direction and is worth having — 向前 forward, 向上 upward,
向左 to the left.

## listen for

Piano and strings, slow, with Chou singing much more clearly than in his
faster material. His diction is often criticised as mumbled; this is a good
counter-example.

Compare with [[gaobai-qiqiu]] to hear the range in one artist: bright bossa nova
against a dark ballad, and quite different vocal placement in each.

Listen for the guitar line that recurs between sections. It functions as the
song's landmark, much as the guzheng figure does in [[ju-hua-tai]].

## patterns

向 + direction | marks direction of movement | 向前走 | walk forward
一路 | the whole way, all along the route | 一路顺风 | have a good trip
越来越 | more and more | 天越来越冷 | It's getting colder and colder
剩下 | to be left over, remaining | 只剩下我一个人 | Only I am left
再也不 | never again | 再也不回头 | never look back again
