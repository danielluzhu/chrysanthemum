## notes

Leehom Wang in a starker mode than [[yiran-ai-ni]]. Where that song is a wedding
standard, this one is about the plain fact of needing someone around.

**Under copyright, so no lyrics here.** Use the video and read the words on a
licensed service.

The title is a compact piece of grammar: 需要 ("to need") + 人 ("a person") + 陪
("to accompany"). Chinese happily strings verbs together like this without the
infinitives or prepositions English would demand — "need person accompany". The
pattern is called a pivot construction and it is everywhere once you notice it.

## listen for

Sparser production than most Wang tracks — the arrangement leaves gaps, which
suits the subject.

Wang's Mandarin is famously precise. He grew up in the United States and learned
much of his Chinese formally, which gives his diction an evenness that native
speakers sometimes describe as slightly textbook. For a learner that is an
advantage.

Listen for the pivot construction recurring. Once you can hear 需要人陪 as three
verbs in a row rather than one lump of sound, a lot of other sentences open up.

## patterns

需要 + person + verb | pivot construction: need someone to do something | 我需要人帮忙 | I need someone to help
陪 | to accompany, to keep company | 你陪我去吧 | Come with me
其實 | actually, in fact | 其实我一点也不好 | Actually I'm not okay at all
一个人 | alone | 一个人吃饭 | eating alone
有时候 | sometimes | 有时候我会想起你 | Sometimes I think of you
