## notes

An early JJ Lin ballad, from the period that established him. Compared with
[[xiulian-aiqing]] the phrasing here is far more spacious — a useful pairing,
because it shows the same singer at two very different speeds.

**Under copyright, so no lyrics here.** Use the video and read the words on a
licensed service.

The title is a bare subordinating conjunction plus a pronoun: 當 ("when") + 你
("you"). Chinese lets a clause hang like that in a way English resists, and
translating it as "When You" keeps the incompleteness deliberately.

## listen for

Slow, piano-led, with a long instrumental introduction. Lin's voice sits in a
comfortable middle register for most of it and only climbs near the end.

Because the tempo is unhurried, this is one of the better JJ Lin tracks for
hearing tone contours survive a melody. Follow the ends of lines especially —
he holds them.

The dynamic build is gradual rather than sudden. Compare it with the abrupt
drop-out in [[tianwai-laiwu]] to hear two opposite ways of handling the same
structural moment.
