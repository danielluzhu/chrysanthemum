## notes

Probably the most memorized poem in the Chinese language — most children in China
learn it before they can write. Twenty characters, four lines, and almost every one
of them is a word a beginner already needs. It has been set to music countless
times, so there are many recordings to pick from.

The whole poem turns on a single physical gesture: head up, head down. Moon, then home.

## lines

床前明月光
Chuáng qián míng yuè guāng
Before my bed, the bright moonlight

疑是地上霜
Yí shì dì shàng shuāng
I took it for frost upon the ground

举头望明月
Jǔ tóu wàng míng yuè
I lift my head and gaze at the bright moon

低头思故乡
Dī tóu sī gùxiāng
I lower my head and think of home

## vocab

明月 | míng yuè | bright moon
霜 | shuāng | frost
举头 | jǔ tóu | to raise one's head
低头 | dī tóu | to lower one's head
故乡 | gùxiāng | hometown, native place
