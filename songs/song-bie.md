## meaning

A farewell at a roadside pavilion, assembled from the standard furniture of Chinese parting poems — long pavilion, ancient road, willows, wine. Li Shutong's plainness is what keeps it from being merely conventional.

## notes

A curious piece of history: the melody is American — Ordway's *Dreaming of Home and
Mother* (1851) — which travelled to Japan, was refitted with Japanese words, and
then reached the monk and artist Li Shutong, who wrote these Chinese lyrics in 1915.
It has been a school-graduation standard in China ever since.

Li Shutong died in 1942 and the melody dates to 1851, so both words and tune are
long out of copyright.

The imagery is stock classical farewell furniture — pavilion, road, willows, wine —
assembled with unusual plainness.

## lines

长亭外
Cháng tíng wài
Beyond the long pavilion

古道边
Gǔ dào biān
alongside the ancient road

芳草碧连天
Fāng cǎo bì lián tiān
sweet grass, green all the way to the sky

晚风拂柳笛声残
Wǎn fēng fú liǔ dí shēng cán
Evening wind stirs the willows; the flute note dies away

夕阳山外山
Xī yáng shān wài shān
sunset, and mountains beyond the mountains

天之涯
Tiān zhī yá
To the ends of the sky

地之角
Dì zhī jiǎo
to the corners of the earth

知交半零落
Zhī jiāo bàn líng luò
half the friends I knew have scattered and fallen away

一壶浊酒尽余欢
Yī hú zhuó jiǔ jìn yú huān
One jug of cloudy wine — let us finish what joy is left

今宵别梦寒
Jīn xiāo bié mèng hán
tonight the parting dream runs cold
