## notes

高爾宣 OSN is one of the most-represented artists on this playlist, and his work
sits in a different place from the ballads around it: Taiwanese R&B and
hip-hop, with English hooks over Mandarin verses.

**Under copyright, so no lyrics here.** Use the video and read the words on a
licensed service.

That mixing is not an affectation — code-switching between Mandarin and English
is ordinary in urban Taiwanese speech, especially among younger speakers, and
pop reflects it. For a learner this is genuinely useful: the English gives you
footholds while the Mandarin around it moves fast.

## listen for

Rapped and sung Mandarin behave differently, and this is a good song to hear
that in. In sung lines the melody largely overrides the tones. In rapped
sections the tones survive much better, because pitch is not being dictated by a
tune. Compare the two within this one track.

The English title and hook recur as anchors. Use them to locate yourself, then
work outward into the Mandarin either side.

The production is sparse — mostly beat, bass and voice — so there is little
competing with the vocal. That makes it more approachable than the density
suggests.

## patterns

沒有你 | without you — 沒有 negates possession or existence | 没有你我不行 | I can't do it without you
Code-switching | English nouns and phrases dropped into Mandarin syntax, very common in Taipei speech | 我今天很busy | I'm very busy today
就 | then, right away — links cause to effect, extremely common | 你来我就走 | If you come, I'll go
一个人 | alone, by oneself | 我一个人住 | I live alone
還是 | still; or — context decides which | 你还是走了 | You left anyway
