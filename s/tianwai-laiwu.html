天外來物

Tiānwài Láiwù

Object from Beyond

Joker Xue 薛之謙 · contemporary

Vocabulary onlyadvanced

The third and most recent of the Joker Xue tracks here, and the strangest. 天外 來物 means an object arriving from beyond the sky — the phrase used for meteorites and, loosely, for aliens. Applied to a person it means someone who does not belong to your world.

Under copyright, so no lyrics here. Use the video and read the words on a licensed service.

The construction is worth taking apart: 天外 ("beyond the sky") + 來 ("come") + 物 ("thing"). Classical-flavoured compression of exactly the kind [[ju-hua-tai]] uses, dropped into a contemporary pop song.

What to listen for

More elaborate production than [[shenshi]] or [[choubaguai]] — layered synths, a bigger low end, and a wider stereo field. Xue's later work leans this way.

The melody moves in wider intervals than his earlier ballads, which makes the tones harder to track. Leave this one until after the other two.

Listen for how the arrangement thins right out before the final chorus. That sudden emptiness is the song's best moment and a common device worth learning to anticipate.
