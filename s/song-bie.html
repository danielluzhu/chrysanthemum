送别

Sòng Bié

Farewell

李叔同 (Li Shutong), to a melody by John P. Ordway · 1915

Full textintermediate

Plays from YouTube · open there instead

A farewell at a roadside pavilion, assembled from the standard furniture of Chinese parting poems — long pavilion, ancient road, willows, wine. Li Shutong's plainness is what keeps it from being merely conventional.

A curious piece of history: the melody is American — Ordway's *Dreaming of Home and Mother* (1851) — which travelled to Japan, was refitted with Japanese words, and then reached the monk and artist Li Shutong, who wrote these Chinese lyrics in 1915. It has been a school-graduation standard in China ever since.

Li Shutong died in 1942 and the melody dates to 1851, so both words and tune are long out of copyright.

The imagery is stock classical farewell furniture — pavilion, road, willows, wine — assembled with unusual plainness.

Xī yáng shān wài shān
sunset, and mountains beyond the mountains
天之涯
Tiān zhī yá
To the ends of the sky
地之角
Dì zhī jiǎo
to the corners of the earth
知交半零落
Zhī jiāo bàn líng luò
half the friends I knew have scattered and fallen away
一壶浊酒尽余欢
Yī hú zhuó jiǔ jìn yú huān
One jug of cloudy wine — let us finish what joy is left
今宵别梦寒
Jīn xiāo bié mèng hán
tonight the parting dream runs cold
