月亮代表我的心

Yuè Liàng Dài Biǎo Wǒ De Xīn

The Moon Represents My Heart

邓丽君 (Teresa Teng) · 1977

Vocabulary onlybeginner

Teresa Teng's 1977 recording is probably the single most recognized Chinese love song anywhere in the world. Slow, gentle, and sung with unusually clear articulation — which makes it a favourite of learners.

Under copyright, so no lyrics here. Listen via the link with the lyrics open on a licensed service alongside.

Grammatically it is a gift for beginners: short sentences, present tense, heavy repetition, and the core pattern 代表 ("stands for / represents") repeated throughout. Learn the words below and you will catch a large share of the song on first listen.

What to listen for

The song is built as a conversation. The verses pose questions and answer them, and that question-then-answer shape repeats often enough that once you catch it in the first verse you can follow the rest structurally, even where individual words escape you.

Teng sings well behind the beat and separates her syllables unusually clearly, which is why teachers reach for this recording. Tones survive mostly intact — rare in sung Chinese, where melody usually overrides them. Try to hear the falling fourth tone on 月 and the way it sits against the descending line.

The title phrase is the hook and returns at the end of each chorus. Count how many times you hear it; that alone will orient you in the structure.

Two minutes fifty or so, verse-chorus-verse-chorus with no bridge. Nothing hurries. It is about as gentle an introduction to listening in Chinese as exists.

Grammar patterns

A 代表 B
A represents / stands for B — the frame the whole song hangs on
这份礼物代表我的谢意 This gift is a token of my thanks
你问我…
"you ask me…" — sets up the question-and-answer shape of the verses
你问我为什么留下来 You ask me why I stayed
有多少
how much, how many — quantity question, no measure word needed
你有多少时间？ How much time do you have?
轻轻地 + verb
reduplicated adjective + 地 makes an adverb: softly, gently
他轻轻地关上门 He closed the door softly
一点点
a little bit — diminutive, softer than 一点
我只会说一点点中文 I speak only a little Chinese
