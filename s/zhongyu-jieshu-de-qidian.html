終於結束的起點

Zhōngyú Jiéshù de Qǐdiǎn

The Starting Point That Finally Ended

Mayday 五月天 · contemporary

Vocabulary onlyadvanced

The second Mayday track here, alongside [[turan-hao-xiang-ni]], and the harder of the two. The title is a small paradox — "the starting point that finally ended" — and the grammar behind it is the reason to study this song.

Under copyright, so no lyrics here. Use the video and read the words on a licensed service.

終於結束的 is a whole clause acting as a modifier on 起點. Chinese builds these left-branching descriptions freely: everything that describes a noun sits in front of it, however long. English would need a relative clause trailing behind. Getting comfortable with front-loaded modifiers is one of the real thresholds in reading Chinese.

What to listen for

A fuller rock arrangement than [[turan-hao-xiang-ni]] — guitars carry it rather than piano, and the band plays as a band throughout instead of building from nothing.

Ashin's delivery is more clipped here, phrases shorter and more urgent. That makes individual words easier to isolate but the overall pace harder to hold.

Listen for the guitar figure that opens the song returning at the end. Mayday use that bookending device often, and it is a useful thing to listen for structurally.

Grammar patterns

Clause + 的 + noun
modifiers go BEFORE the noun, however long the clause
终于结束的旅程 the journey that finally ended
終於
finally, at last — after a long wait
我终于明白了 I finally understand
結束
to end, to finish
一切都结束了 It's all over
起點
starting point
这是新的起点 This is a new beginning
既是…也是…
both … and …
这既是结束也是开始 This is both an ending and a beginning
