菊花台

Jú Huā Tái

Chrysanthemum Terrace

周杰伦 (Jay Chou), lyrics by 方文山 (Vincent Fang) · 2006

Vocabulary onlyadvanced

The song this project is named after. Written for the film *Curse of the Golden Flower*, with lyrics by Vincent Fang in the ornate pseudo-classical register he made his signature — a soldier at the frontier, a woman waiting, and chrysanthemums as the image of loss.

What to listen for

Fang writes in a deliberately archaic register — the vocabulary and grammar of classical poetry set against a modern ballad arrangement. That mismatch is the whole effect, and it is also why this song is hard: the words are often one syllable where modern Mandarin would use two, and the particles are literary ones you will not meet in conversation.

The arrangement tells you where you are even when the words do not. Listen for the guzheng figure that opens it and returns between sections, the erhu answering the vocal line, and the strings that swell only in the chorus. Verse sections sit low and narrow; the chorus climbs and opens out. That contour is your map.

The situation behind it: a soldier at the frontier, someone waiting at home, and the chrysanthemum as the image binding them — a flower associated in Chinese tradition with autumn, mourning, and remembrance rather than the cheerfulness it suggests in English.

Come back to this one after the easier songs. The vocabulary below is the barrier, not the speed.

Grammar patterns

已
literary "already" — replaces modern 已经, one syllable instead of two
天色已暗 The sky has already darkened
无
literary negation — replaces 没有 in poetic register
无人知晓 No one knows
如
literary "like, as" — replaces 像
时光如水 Time is like flowing water
一地 + noun
"a whole ground covered in —" — quantity by area, very visual
一地落叶 Leaves all over the ground
Monosyllabic nouns
classical verse compresses two-syllable words to one: 月 for 月亮, 花 for 花朵
月下独行 Walking alone beneath the moon
